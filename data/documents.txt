Einstein developed the special theory of relativity in 1905. This groundbreaking work showed that the laws of physics are the same for all non-accelerating observers, and that the speed of light in a vacuum is the same no matter the speed at which an observer travels. As a result, he found that space and time were interwoven into a single continuum known as space-time. Events that occur at the same time for one observer could occur at different times for another.

The general theory of relativity, published in 1915, extended special relativity to include gravity. Einstein proposed that massive objects cause a distortion in space-time, which is felt as gravity. This was a radical departure from Newton's conception of gravity as a force acting at a distance. The theory predicted phenomena such as the bending of light around massive objects, the existence of black holes, and gravitational waves.

Pasta is one of the most versatile foods in Italian cuisine. The key to great pasta is in the water — it should be heavily salted, almost like seawater. The pasta should be cooked al dente, meaning it should still have a slight bite to it. Different shapes of pasta are suited to different sauces — long thin pasta like spaghetti works well with light oil-based sauces, while short chunky pasta like rigatoni holds up well to thick meat sauces.

The water cycle, also known as the hydrological cycle, describes the continuous movement of water through Earth's systems. Water evaporates from oceans and lakes, rises into the atmosphere, condenses into clouds, and falls back to Earth as precipitation. This cycle is driven by solar energy and gravity. It plays a crucial role in distributing heat around the planet and supporting all life on Earth.
